# WYRDBOUND — Saga Bible

The reference for the story, the cast and the shape of the series. When writing new dialogue, check it against this document.

## The premise

Loki engineered the death of Baldr the Bright. The gods' revenge reached his family:
- They turned Loki's son **Váli** into a wolf.
- The wolf killed his brother **Narfi**.
- They bound Loki beneath the earth with Narfi's entrails, which then turned to iron.
- **Skaði** hung a serpent above Loki's face. His wife **Sigyn** catches its venom in a bowl. When she turns to empty it, the poison falls and the earth quakes.

Narfi wakes dead in **Éljúðnir**, the hall of his half-sister **Hel**. He can hear his father screaming through the stone, so he climbs. Every death returns him to the Pool of Return, and every return moves the story forward.

## The central truth, revealed slowly

Narfi is not the key to his father's prison. **He is the lock.** The chains are his thread, his *wyrd*, made iron. Cutting them unravels him. That is why the dead cannot linger on the surface: the binding pulls him home.

Odin knew all of it. He made Váli a wolf deliberately ("This will hurt, boy, but it will matter") because the gods needed a binding no god could untie. He means to have it broken at exactly the right moment, the one the völva foretold, so that Ragnarök unfolds the way he has seen it. **Mímir's head in Hel's hall is his spy.**

## The route of an escape

| Realm | Character | Warden (boss) |
|---|---|---|
| **Niflhel**, the Mist-Depths | The oathless dead as draugr, mist-wraiths, bone-rats. Cold, blue, quiet. | **Váli**, the Wolf-Brother |
| **Náströnd**, the Corpse-Shore | A hall woven of serpents, venom and murderers. Sickly green. | **Níðhöggr**, Gnawer of the Root |
| **Gjöll** and Gjallarbrú | The river of knives and the echoing bridge. Steel and snow. | **Garmr**, the blood-chested hound of Gnipahellir |
| **Helgrind**, the Gate of the Dead | Obsidian and purple flame. Hel's elite. | **Hel** herself, who has two lives |
| **The Surface** (Midgard) | The cave of binding: Loki, Sigyn, the serpent. | — |

Mini-bosses: Hrafnkell the Unburied (a Grave-Jarl), Gríðr of the Woven Hall (the Serpent-Weaver) and the Warden of Gjallarbrú.

## The cast

### The hall
- **Hel**: the Hades analog, and Narfi's half-sister. Cold at first and loving underneath. She fears Ragnarök because her people, the forgotten dead she gave a home, would sail out on Naglfar and die again. She opposes the escape out of love, not cruelty. Keepsake: the Half-Mask.
- **Ganglöt**: Hel's servant, named for slowness (as in the myth). She keeps the Book of Deaths and greets every return with a comment on how Narfi died. She speaks... very... slowly. She is the comic heart of the hall.
- **Baldr**: the murdered god. He forgave Loki's son on sight and teaches combat. **Twist:** he is the god who benefits most from Ragnarök, because he returns after it, and he admits he has "counted the years." Keepsake: Draupnir's Echo.
- **Nanna**: Baldr's wife, who died of grief, or rather of *choosing*. Motherly. She remembers Sigyn, and her message to Sigyn is a small subplot that pays off. Keepsake: Linen.
- **Mímir**: the head and keeper of the Codex. Wry and evasive. **Odin's spy.** Arc: a raven at the gate leads to suspicion, then a confession ("It was not cruelty. It was carpentry"), then partial redemption ("I have stopped telling the ravens everything. Only most things."). Keepsake: Mímir's Draught.
- **The Norns**: Urðr, Verðandi and Skuld. They appear at the hall's well after the first escape and run the Weave of Wyrd (heat). They deliver the central truth: "You are the lock."
- **Váli**: Niflhel's boss. He regains words across repeated defeats. After three, Hel brings him home to her fire. Gifts of mead earn his Fang and, eventually, Narfi's forgiveness ("Say it again." / "I don't hate you, brother."). He also carries the memory of Odin's visit before the curse.

### Met in the depths
- **Móðguðr**: keeper of Gjallarbrú and shopkeeper. Dry. The bridge never rings for the dead, until one day it rings, faintly, for Narfi. Keepsake: Toll-Stone.
- **Höðr**: Baldr's blind brother and killer. Narfi carries Baldr's forgiveness back to him.
- **Hermóðr**: who rode to Hel for Baldr. He reveals that Odin once asked him whether "the trickster's son still breathes", long before Narfi died.

### On the surface
- **Loki**: charming, guilty, manipulative and occasionally sincere ("It's the first thing I've been sorry for in a thousand years").
- **Sigyn**: Narfi's mother and the emotional core. She reveals Odin's visit ("Not yet.") and gives the saga's thesis: *"Don't give him his ending, my love. Find your own."* Keepsake: a chip of the Bowl.

### The gods who bless him, and why
| God | Mechanic | Motive |

## The ending of Saga I

It is unlocked after **4+ escapes**, *the Norns' truth*, *Mímir's confession* and *Váli's forgiveness*. At the cave, Narfi chooses:

- **Cut the bonds.** Loki is freed, Narfi unravels, Fenrir's chain snaps, Jörmungandr wakes, and Naglfar slips its moorings with a green-eyed figure at the prow. → **Saga II: TWILIGHT**
- **Hold the knot.** Narfi becomes the chain forever and returns to Hel's fire beside his brother. Odin's ravens find the cave still closed. The Norns smile: *"Threads can be cut by other hands."* → **Saga II: THE OTHER HANDS**

The game continues after either ending (heat, runs, unseen dialogue). Hel acknowledges the choice.

## Tone rules for new writing

- Myth told from the inside: grief, family and fate, never Marvel.
- Every character is funny in their own way, but no one is a joke.
- Short lines. The dead are tired; let the silences do work.
- Hel never begs. Odin never apologizes. Ganglöt never hurries.
- Use the tokens `{cause}`, `{runs}`, `{clears}` and `{deaths}` to make returns personal.

## Where the series goes

- **Saga II** follows the chosen ending. *Twilight*: Narfi's unravelled thread drifts through Ragnarök itself, on Naglfar and the field of Vígríðr. *The Other Hands*: Váli and Hel seek another way to cut the knot without destroying Narfi, against Odin's design.
- **Saga III** (working title *The Green World*): after the end, in the world that rises from the sea. Baldr's hope, tested.
